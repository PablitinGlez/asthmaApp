Abstract—The missing persons crisis represents a 
significant humanitarian challenge, with a critical sub-problem 
in the identification of unlocated patients within health 
institutions. This paper presents IAPEX (Artificial Intelligence 
for the Localization of Missing Patients in Health Institutions), 
a validated software system that addresses this issue through a 
novel hybrid search algorithm. The solution integrates textual 
data analysis with facial recognition to maximize sensitivity 
and minimize operational load. The system uses a 
multi-algorithm approach for pattern matching in textual 
descriptions and a pre-trained convolutional neural network 
(CNN) for facial image analysis. Both components are 
combined through an optimized weighting system that 
generates a global similarity score. Experimental tests show 
that the system achieves a Sensitivity (Recall) of 100% in all its 
operating modes, ensuring that no person is missed. 
Furthermore, it achieves a perfect Effectiveness (Accuracy) of 
100% in its image and hybrid modes, and 96.8% in text mode, 
validating its high reliability and minimal false positives. 
Keywords—facial recognition, natural language processing, 
person location, hybrid system, convolutional neural networks, 
IAPEX, pattern matching, confusion matrix, sensitivity analysis. 
I. 
INTRODUCTION 
Mexico is facing a severe humanitarian crisis regarding 
missing persons, with figures exceeding 100,000 national 
records [1]. Within this universe, there is an 
often-underestimated subset of cases: patients who are 
admitted to health institutions (hospitals, clinics, shelters) 
without identification documents or in a state that prevents 
them from communicating their identity. These individuals, 
victims of accidents, mental health crises, or violence, 
remain in an administrative and emotional limbo while their 
families actively search for them. 
Current localization methods depend on manual 
processes, telephone interactions, and the dissemination of 
information on social media. While the latter has proven to 
be an invaluable channel for citizen aid, they lack the 
structure, centralization, and speed necessary to constitute a 
systematic solution. Fragmented information and the 
absence of a specialized technological tool prolong anguish 
and consume valuable resources. 
To address this gap, IAPEX has been developed, a 
validated system whose core is a hybrid identification 
algorithm. This algorithm combines the analysis of textual 
descriptions (morphological data, clothing, particular signs) 
with facial recognition technology to create a centralized 
and efficient search system, designed with a non-negotiable 
requirement: to never fail in finding an existing match. 
The objective of this paper is to present in detail the 
architecture, validation methodology, and quantitative 
results of this hybrid algorithm. It will describe how the 
system weights textual and visual information to calculate a 
similarity score and how, through an iterative optimization 
process, a performance was achieved that guarantees 
operational viability without sacrificing sensitivity. 
II. 
RELATED WORK 
The application of technology in the search for missing 
persons has been a field of growing interest. The existing 
technological landscape includes solutions ranging from 
public web repositories to specialized biometric systems and 
real-time notification protocols. 
Several initiatives, such as FindMe by Combita et al. [2] 
and Encuéntrame RD by Vargas et al. [3], have focused on 
creating centralized web repositories. These platforms are 
crucial for social mobilization and public consultation, 
allowing for the widespread dissemination of information 
about missing individuals. However, their primary function 
is information hosting, and they generally lack proactive 
intelligent analysis capabilities for automatic identification. 
In the biometric domain, the work of Pedroza Manga [4] 
stands out for its implementation of a system based purely 
on facial biometrics using a Convolutional Neural Network 
(CNN). This approach focuses on the direct comparison of 
facial images for identification, offering high precision when 
a clear photo is available but remaining ineffective if the 
only available information is a textual description. 
Pushing the biometric approach further, Annamalai et al. 
[9] explored the use of advanced Deep Learning techniques 
not just for matching faces but also for facial reconstruction. 
This adds a layer of sophistication to visual analysis but 
remains highly dependent on visual input and does not 
address search scenarios based solely on text.

The IAPEX system differs from previous approaches by 
proposing a validated hybrid model. Unlike web 
repositories, it incorporates an intelligent search engine. In 
contrast to purely biometric systems, IAPEX is functional 
even with partial or purely textual information. Finally, 
while systems like SIGPRO focus on communication, 
IAPEX focuses on the accuracy of identification. This 
fusion of modalities, optimized for 100% sensitivity, makes 
it more robust and adaptable to real-world conditions.

scalability and a clear separation of responsibilities. The 
architecture defines the flow of information from the 
user-facing client applications to the data storage and 
processing backend. Figure 1 provides a high-level

C. Data Layer 
This layer is responsible for the persistent storage of all 
system information, strategically divided into two 
specialized databases for optimal performance. 
● PostgreSQL DB (Main Database): This relational 
database stores all structured information of the 
patients, such as their names, physical descriptions, 
medical conditions, and registration status. The 
Textual Module performs its queries against this 
database. 
● MongoDB (Face Encodings DB): This NoSQL 
database is used as a high-performance facial index. 
It stores the numerical face encodings (128-point 
vectors) associated with each patient's ID. This 
specialization allows the Facial Module to perform 
thousands of comparisons in milliseconds, a task for 
which MongoDB is much more efficient than a 
traditional relational database. 
IV. 
METHODOLOGY AND ALGORITHM DESIGN 
The IAPEX algorithm is designed as a hybrid system 
that calculates a similarity score by combining two analysis 
streams: one for textual data and one for visual data. The 
architecture is implemented in Python and exposed through 
a RESTful API built with Django. 
A. Textual Analysis Module 
This module compares the text fields of the query (name, 
gender, etc.) with patient records in the PostgreSQL 
database. To maximize accuracy, a multi-algorithm approach 
is implemented, including Levenshtein, Jaro-Winkler, and 
Soundex. 
1. Textual Weighting System: A system of 
configurable weights for each descriptive field was 
implemented. The total textual score (Simtext ) is 
the weighted sum of the similarities. 
B. Visual Analysis Module (Facial Recognition) 
This module is activated if the user provides images. The 
robustness of pre-trained models is leveraged through the 
face_recognition library [8]. 
1. Underlying Library: The library acts as an interface 
to dlib [7], which contains a pre-trained CNN for 
facial recognition. The model projects a face into a 
128-dimensional vector space. 
2. Face Encodings Extraction: For each face, the 
system extracts its face encoding, a vector of 128 
numbers representing its unique features. 
3. Facial Similarity Calculation: The similarity 
between two faces is calculated as the Euclidean 
distance between their respective vectors. A smaller 
distance implies greater similarity. 
C. Hybrid Module and Total Score Calculation 
The final score is calculated by combining the results of 
both modules. After an optimization process, the weights 
were rebalanced to give more importance to facial 
recognition, which demonstrated superior accuracy. The 
formula is as follows: 
SimTotal =(Simtext ×0.25)+(Simfacial ×0.75) (1) 
A patient is considered a match if their SimTotal exceeds 
a predefined threshold, which was rigorously adjusted 
during the experimentation phase. 
D. Multi-Stage Filtering System 
To reduce false positives without compromising 
sensitivity, a three-stage filter was implemented to process 
the raw results: 
1. Base Threshold Filter: Discards candidates with 
scores below a strict threshold, specific to each 
search mode (text, image, hybrid). 
2. Feature Consensus: Evaluates the coherence 
between multiple matching fields to penalize 
coincidental matches. 
3. Results Limit: Limits the number of final candidates 
to avoid overloading the human operator, showing 
only the most relevant ones.

A. 
Dataset 
Since a false negative is unacceptable for this type of 
system, having a perfect ground truth was imperative. 
Therefore, a synthetic dataset of 310 patients was generated 
using the Faker library for realistic demographic data and 
faces generated by GANs via ThisPersonDoesNotExist.com. 
The dataset consisted of: 
● 50 Target Patients: Base cases for evaluation. 
● 250 Distractor Patients: Realistic negative cases to 
generate noise and measure precision. 
● 10 Duplicates with Variations: Cases of target 
patients with slightly altered data and photos to test 
the system's robustness (see TABLE II). 
This approach ensured a controlled and ethical testing 
environment without using real personal data. A total of 
3,720 evaluations were performed in the final validation, 
based on 12 known positive cases (TP + FN) and 3,708 
known negative cases (FP + TN).

To demonstrate the system's complete workflow, this 
section illustrates how the hybrid model processes a query. 
The system is designed for robustness under real-world 
conditions and accepts images in JPG and PNG formats with 
a maximum size of 10 MB and a minimum resolution of 
500x500 pixels. While ideal images are clear, well-lit, and 
front-facing, the algorithm is built to handle suboptimal 
photos, such as those that might be the only ones available 
to a searching family member. In a medical context, where 
photos can be taken up close, the system's resilience is key, 
as a patient's face might be partially obscured by bandages, 
tubes, or injuries. The model's ability to generate a reliable 
match despite these potential occlusions is a critical feature.

VII. 
DISCUSSION 
The experimental results demonstrate the superior 
effectiveness of the hybrid approach. The image and hybrid 
modes achieved perfect performance (100% on all metrics), 
indicating that with a photograph, the system is infallible in 
both detection and precision. 
The text mode's performance, particularly at the optimal 
75% threshold, confirms its viability. Although a Precision 
of 9.2% might seem low, it must be interpreted within the 
framework of a guaranteed 100% Sensitivity (Recall). As 
shown in the confusion matrix (TABLE V), the system 
successfully achieved its primary objective: zero False 
Negatives (FN=0). This configuration ensures no potential 
match is missed and reduces the False Positives to 118, 
translating into a manageable operational load of 
approximately 10 manual reviews per real match. The 
system's high Effectiveness (Accuracy) of 96.8% in this 
mode further validates its overall reliability even when only 
textual information is available. 
A. Limitations and Future Work 
The main limitation of this validation is the use of a 
synthetic dataset. Although methodologically necessary for 
rigor, the next step must be validation in a controlled 
environment with anonymized real data. Furthermore, the 
performance of the text mode, while operationally viable, 
can still be improved. 
Three main lines are proposed for future work: 
1. Replace the current text algorithms with more 
advanced Natural Language Processing (NLP) 
models, such as transformers (e.g., BERT), to 
perform semantic searches and further reduce the 
remaining 118 false positives. 
2. Explore the fine-tuning of the CNN model with a 
dataset specific to the hospital context to improve its 
robustness against low-quality images. 
VIII. 
CONCLUSION 
This paper has presented the design, validation 
methodology, and results of IAPEX, a hybrid AI system for 
identifying missing patients in health institutions. The core 
of the system is an algorithm that combines weighted textual 
search and facial recognition. 
The experimental results, supported by mathematical 
formulas, performance metrics, and confusion matrices, 
show that the hybrid approach not only achieves a perfect 
F1-Score of 100% when images are available but also 
maintains a perfect Sensitivity (Recall) of 100% in its 
text-only mode, validating its reliability in the most critical 
scenario. The system's high Effectiveness (Accuracy) further 
solidifies this conclusion. 
The main contribution of this work is the formalization 
and empirical validation of a pragmatic system that merges 
two data modalities to solve a pressing social problem. 
IAPEX represents a significant step towards the automation 
and streamlining of search processes, transforming a manual 
and error-prone task into an efficient, accurate, and, above 
all, humane technological process.
