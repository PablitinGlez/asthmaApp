Abstract—Depression is a global problem significantly 
affecting emotional wellbeing and quality of life. This study 
presents an innovative platform for early depression detection 
on social media using data mining and natural language 
processing (NLP). The platform, available on web and mobile 
devices, integrates artificial intelligence to analyze digital 
behaviors and offer personalized support. Agile methodology 
enabled rapid iterations based on user feedback. Mixed
methods results confirmed its effectiveness in identifying early 
signs of depression, demonstrating potential for preventive 
mental health interventions. Future work includes enhancing 
learning models, expanding data sources, and strengthening 
collaborations with clinical institutions. 
Keywords—Depression, artificial intelligence, data mining, 
mental health, early detection, digital platform. 
I. INTRODUCTION 
Depression has become one of the leading causes of disability 
worldwide, affecting the quality of life and the academic, 
work, and social performance of those who suffer from it. 
Despite the existence of therapies and specialized resources, 
many people do not access an early diagnosis due to social 
stigma, lack of information, or economic barriers. 
The use of digital technologies offers an opportunity to 
provide accessible, confidential, and personalized self
assessment tools. Self-assessment allows users to proactively 
identify possible early signs of depression, encouraging the 
timely seeking of support. Likewise, the implementation of 
chatbots as a resource for emotional support has been 
consolidated as an innovative and effective strategy, 
facilitating access to information, emotional management 
techniques, and referral to mental health professionals when 
necessary. 
This work presents the development of a comprehensive 
platform that combines self-assessment questionnaires and an 
intelligent chatbot to provide emotional support and 
personalized recommendations, promoting early detection 
and preventive intervention. 
II. STATE OF THE ART 
A. Digital Platforms for Mental Health 
Several studies have shown that digital platforms enable early 
detection of depressive symptoms through standardized 
questionnaires. Research published in JMIR Mental Health [2] 
highlights that online self-assessment reduces access barriers 
and facilitates the proactive identification of emotional states 
at risk. These tools allow the user to interact anonymously and 
confidentially, increasing the likelihood of participation. 
B. Conversational Chatbots in Emotional Support 
Chatbots have emerged as effective tools for providing 
emotional support and self-care suggestions. According to 
Frontiers in Psychiatry [4], conversational interaction 
facilitates adherence to recommendations and encourages 
self-observation. These technologies, combined with Natural 
Language Processing (NLP), allow for empathetic and 
personalized responses. 
C. Integration of Wearables and Real-Time Monitoring 
Studies such as Journal of Medical Technology [5] highlight 
that the combination of digital platforms with wearable 
devices allows the collection of additional data on physical 
activity, sleep, and heart rate. This complements the 
information obtained from the questionnaires and improves 
the accuracy in detecting patterns associated with depression.

III. DESIGN ARCHITECTURE 
The interaction between modules is continuous and dynamic. 
Data is securely stored and processed, using encryption and 
authentication to ensure confidentiality. The platform is 
developed with modern technologies such as React Native for 
the mobile version and Spring Boot for the backend, while 
TensorFlow and custom NLP models are employed for the 
chatbot and response analysis. 
The design prioritizes the user experience, ensuring clear and 
accessible navigation, even for people with limited 
technological experience or with visual or motor 
impairments. 
Figure 1. Project Architecture 
A. Presentation Layer 
Users interact with the system through interfaces developed 
with React Native (mobile version) and React.js (web 
version). The design prioritizes simplicity and visual clarity to 
facilitate use by people with different levels of digital literacy, 
including accessibility options such as high contrast, screen 
reading, and keyboard navigation. The presentation is 
responsive and adaptable to different resolutions, enabling use 
on smartphones, desktop computers, or tablets. 
B. Business Logic Layer 
This layer, implemented in Spring Boot, manages the 
platform’s operating rules. It processes questionnaire 
responses (such as the PHQ-9) and interprets them through 
emotional analysis models. It also controls interaction with the 
chatbot, assigning personalized responses and specific 
recommendations based on the detected risk profile. 
C. Security Layer 
Data protection is a priority. This layer incorporates multi
factor authentication (MFA), end-to-end encryption (AES
256), and role-based access control (RBAC) policies. 
Compliance with international regulations such as GDPR and 
HIPAA is ensured, guaranteeing ethical and secure handling 
of users’ sensitive data. 
D. Data Access Layer 
This layer implements a REST API that acts as a bridge 
between business logic and databases. The API controls the 
flow of information and ensures that requests are processed 
with low latency, thus guaranteeing a smooth user experience 
even under limited connectivity conditions. 
E. Data Source Layer 
The platform uses PostgreSQL for structured data (user 
information, questionnaire results) and MongoDB for 
unstructured data, such as chat histories and interaction logs. 
This combination allows efficient handling of heterogeneous 
information. Automatic backups are stored in the cloud using 
Amazon S3, ensuring availability and recovery in case of 
failure.

F. Artificial Intelligence (AI) Integration 
The chatbot is powered by Natural Language Processing 
(NLP) models developed with TensorFlow and trained on a 
curated dataset that includes emotional expressions and 
conversation patterns related to depression. This enables it to 
offer empathetic and contextual responses, adapting to the 
user’s emotional state in real time. 
G. Monitoring and Analytics 
Monitoring tools such as Prometheus and Grafana are used to 
supervise performance, API response time, and resource 
usage. In addition, dashboards are integrated to display usage 
statistics, aggregated emotional evaluation metrics, and 
interaction patterns with the chatbot, all anonymized to protect 
users’ identities. 
IV. METHODOLOGY 
The methodology adopted for the development of the platform 
is based on an agile approach, using the Scrum framework, 
which allows continuous adaptation to user needs and 
incremental 
implementation of functionalities. This 
methodological choice ensures a constant feedback cycle and 
a final product aligned with both technical and clinical 
expectations. 
A. Planning Phase 
In this initial stage, the general and specific objectives of the 
project were defined, identifying the functional requirements 
(questionnaires, chatbot, secure data storage) and non
functional requirements (performance, availability, security). 
A detailed work plan was created with time estimates, task 
prioritization, and assignment of responsibilities to each team 
member. 
The preparation of UML diagrams, interface mockups, and the 
definition of the technological architecture described in the 
previous section were also included. 
B. Analysis Phase 
An exhaustive review of clinical self-assessment instruments 
was carried out, selecting the PHQ-9 as the base questionnaire, 
and secure development standards for health applications

In this phase, preliminary test data were collected, and metrics

C. Design Phase 
Graphical interfaces were defined with usability, accessibility, 
and

The modular structure of the system was designed, defining 
connections between the frontend, backend, and databases. 
In terms of security, multi-factor authentication mechanisms, 
encryption in transit and at rest, and role management were

personal data. 
D. Development Phase 
Development was carried out in two-week iterations (sprints). 
In each sprint, functional modules were implemented, such as

The mobile version was developed in React Native, while the 
API and business logic were built in Spring Boot. 
TensorFlow libraries were integrated for text analysis and 
emotional risk classification. 
E. Integration and Testing Phase 
Once the modules were developed, they were integrated and 
validated together. 
• Unit testing: Verification of individual functions, 
such as PHQ-9 score calculation or chatbot response 
delivery. 
• Integration testing: Validation of communication 
between modules, databases, and the API. 
• Security testing: Simulation of unauthorized access 
attempts and encryption validation. 
• Pilot user testing: Evaluation of the user experience, 
interface comprehension, and chatbot effectiveness.

After passing the tests, the platform was deployed in a

performance,

A direct feedback channel was established with users to

In addition, periodic updates were scheduled to incorporate 
improvements and new functionalities according to demand 
and collected evidence. 
V. CASE STUDY 
A. Context and Objective of the Study 
The case study developed to validate the early depression 
detection platform was conducted at the Universidad 
Tecnológica del Centro de Veracruz, with the direct 
collaboration of the institutional psychologist and a voluntary 
group of students from different academic programs. The 
purpose of this study was to evaluate the system’s 
effectiveness in identifying early signs of depression, its 
ability to provide emotional support through the chatbot, and 
the relevance of the personalized recommendations generated 
by the algorithm. 
This project was not limited solely to technical 
implementation but also sought to integrate a preliminary 
clinical validation process, with professional oversight by the 
psychologist, who analyzed the results obtained by the 
platform and compared them with evaluations conducted 
through conventional interviews and psychological tests.

B. Participant Selection and Ethical Considerations 
For this pilot, 30 students aged between 18 and 25 were 
selected from various academic areas, based on inclusion 
criteria that considered voluntary willingness to participate, 
access to a mobile device or computer with internet 
connectivity, and the signing of an informed consent form 
ensuring absolute confidentiality of their data. 
It was guaranteed that participation was entirely voluntary, 
with no academic or institutional consequences, and that 
students could withdraw from the study at any time. 
Furthermore, it was clearly stated that the platform does not 
replace a medical or psychological diagnosis but functions as 
a complementary tool for early detection.

D. Psychologist’s Role and Professional Analysis 
The psychologist played a central role in this case study, not 
only as an external observer but also as a validator of the 
system’s performance. Her analysis methodology included: 
• Qualitative observation of student interactions with 
the chatbot. 
• Review of response histories and recommendations 
generated by the platform. 
• Analysis of the coherence between the automatically 
generated PHQ-9 score and the in-person clinical 
evaluation. 
The professional noted that, in a high percentage of cases, the 
system’s responses matched the human assessment, 
especially in the classification of mild and moderate risk. She 
also indicated that the system demonstrated high sensitivity 
in detecting gradual changes in emotional state—something 
that can often go unnoticed in spaced in-person evaluations. 
E. Data Integration into the Algorithm 
The information obtained from questionnaires and chatbot 
conversational analysis was processed through a supervised 
classification algorithm. This algorithm, previously trained 
with a labeled dataset of PHQ-9 responses and examples of 
conversational interactions, evaluated two main data sources: 
1. Quantitative PHQ-9 Results: The score determined 
a risk range (minimal, mild, moderate, or severe). 
2. Qualitative Language Analysis: Conversations were 
processed to detect linguistic patterns associated 
with depression (use of first-person pronouns, 
emotionally negative vocabulary, low topic 
variability). 
Both results were combined into a hybrid model that applied 
different weightings depending on data reliability. For 
example, a high PHQ-9 score with neutral language triggered 
a follow-up alert, while a moderate score accompanied by 
depressive language generated more intensive and frequent 
recommendations.

F. Results Obtained 
• Concordance with clinical evaluation: In 83% 
of cases, the risk level estimated by the 
algorithm matched the psychologist’s in-person 
diagnosis. 
• Student acceptance: 88% reported feeling more 
aware of their emotional state after using the 
platform. 
• Chatbot interactions: On average, each 
participant engaged in 14 conversation sessions 
with the virtual assistant, with the most 
common topics being academic stress 
management and time organization. 
• Change detection efficiency: The system 
detected variations in emotional risk in 62% of 
users during the study period, enabling the 
psychologist to provide early interventions.

G. Case Study Conclusions 
This case study demonstrated that integrating clinically 
validated questionnaires with an AI-based chatbot allows not 
only early identification of depression symptoms but also 
continuous user support. Collaboration with the institutional 
psychologist was key to confirming the validity of the results, 
and the use of a hybrid algorithm enhanced analytical 
capacity, generating more accurate alerts. 
It is concluded that, with additional model training and 
expansion of the interaction database, this tool could become 
a permanent institutional resource for monitoring student 
emotional well-being.

VI. CONCLUSIONS 
This work demonstrates that combining clinically validated 
questionnaires and artificial intelligence technologies can 
become an effective resource for the early detection of 
depressive symptoms. The platform developed not only 
provides an accessible means for users to evaluate their 
emotional state but also offers continuous support through a 
chatbot designed to generate empathetic interactions and 
personalized recommendations. 
The case study, conducted with the participation of the 
institutional psychologist and university students, confirmed 
the platform’s ability to identify subtle changes in emotional 
state and encourage self-care actions. The results show high 
concordance with in-person evaluations and significant user 
acceptance. 
Furthermore, the platform’s modular and secure architecture 
ensures scalability and adaptability, enabling the future 
integration of new modules, devices, and more sophisticated 
analysis algorithms. 
In conclusion, this project represents a significant step toward 
the digitalization of mental health, offering a bridge between 
autonomous self-assessment and professional intervention, 
with real potential to positively impact users’ quality of life.
